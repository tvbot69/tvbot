    "The reason why I can safely say that Radiohead are The Beatles of our generation is because, much like the Fab Four, they represent and portray a natural musical progression. From their Brit-pop roots to their later electronica-influenced soundscapes, Radiohead were always concerned with musical growth and maturation. As a 13-year-old relying on MTV to deliver to me the optimal selection of music out there, finding Radiohead, and seeing their evolution, was imperative to getting me to expand my tastes and listen to good music. The Bends introduced me to rock. OK Computer taught me how music wasn't all just processed immediacy and led me into further exploration. Kid A taught me how to listen to music as an art form. At all of the turning points of my musical listening experiences, Radiohead were there and helped me progress and mature musically. They're essentially the perfect band to grow up with and, like The Beatles, their timeless albums will continue to convert new fans and astound critics for decades to come, which is the most any band can hope to accomplish."

    Biography

    Radiohead, a British Alternative Rock band, has been one of the most successful groups of the 1990s and 2000s, scoring big with critics and building a massive, devoted fanbase. Originally formed in Oxford under the name On a Friday in 1985, they changed their name a few years later in 1991 to Radiohead (taking it from a Talking Heads song) and released an EP, Drill. They eventually gained record label interest, and signed with EMI Records in 1993. Their debut album Pablo Honey came out in the heart of the Grunge era, and as a result they were often lumped in with bands like Nirvana. The album contained their first big hit "Creep". Their second full length album The Bends presented a harder-edged sound which met critical acclaim. Experimentalist music videos for tracks like "Just" and "Street Spirit" played a big part in bringing attention to the band. But it wasn't until OK Computer that their popularity hit its peak. Critics declared it a masterpiece of modern rock, and songs like "Karma Police" received heavy radio play.

    They followed up their most successful album to date with Kid A, which was a departure from their earlier work. More experimental and electronic in nature, it received polarizing reactions from both critics and fans, but has been hailed as a masterpiece in retrospect. They quickly followed it up with 2001's Amnesiac, a collection of songs from the recording sessions of Kid A.

    Jonny Greenwood branched out from the band in 2003, recording Bodysong, an experimental soundtrack for the film of the same name. That same year, Radiohead released Hail to the Thief, a partial return to guitar-driven rock music while still retaining elements of their recent foray into electronic music. In 2006, Thom Yorke released a solo album, The Eraser.

    In 2007, In Rainbows was self-released by Radiohead as a digital download with no set price; later, a standard CD with a bonus disc was released. It was followed up in 2011 by The King of Limbs, a record built around looped rhythms and ambient textures, that was released in a similar fashion.

    In 2016 Radiohead released A Moon Shaped Pool, their first full-length album in over five years. The long interim between records was devoted to various side projects: Jonny Greenwood continued to compose orchestral and film scores, collaborating extensively with director Paul Thomas Anderson, whilst in 2009, Thom Yorke formed the supergroup Atoms for Peace, who recorded the album Amok in 2013. Meanwhile, drummer Philip Selway recorded two solo albums in 2010 and 2014 respectively.
